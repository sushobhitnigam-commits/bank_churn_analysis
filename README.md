# Bank Churn Analysis Using Excel

## Project Overview

Customer churn is a major challenge for banks because losing customers can reduce revenue and long-term customer value. This project analyzes bank customer data using Microsoft Excel to identify churn patterns, understand customer segments, and recommend strategies to improve customer retention.

The project uses Excel PivotTables, calculated columns, churn-rate analysis, and an interactive dashboard to present actionable business insights.

## Business Objectives

- Analyze the bank's overall customer base and churn rate.
- Identify customer segments with higher churn rates.
- Compare churn patterns across demographics and financial characteristics.
- Understand the relationship between customer activity and churn.
- Examine churn rates by number of banking products.
- Recommend strategies to improve customer retention.

## Tools and Technologies

- PivotTables and PivotCharts

## Key Insights

### 1. Age and Customer Churn

| Age Segment | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|
| Under 25 | 7.53% |
| Between 25 and 50 | 17.57% |
| Above 50 | 44.63% |

Customers above 50 have the highest observed churn rate. The bank should investigate their needs and consider personalized retention programs.

### 2. Geographic Churn

| Geography | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|
| France | 16.15% |
| Germany | 32.44% |
| Spain | 16.67% |

Germany has the highest churn rate among the three geographic segments. Further investigation into customer satisfaction, service quality, pricing, and competitor offerings may help explain this difference.

### 3. Customer Activity

| Customer Status | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|
| Inactive | 26.85% |
| Active | 14.27% |

Inactive customers have a churn rate 12.58 percentage points higher than active customers. This suggests that customer engagement should be a priority in retention planning.

### 4. Balance Segmentation

| Balance Segment | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|
| Low | 14.25% |
| Medium | 19.88% |
| High | 25.23% |

Customers in the high-balance segment have a higher observed churn rate than customers in the low-balance segment. The bank could investigate the needs and concerns of these potentially valuable customers.

### 5. Gender Analysis

| Gender | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|
| Female | 25.07% |
| Male | 16.46% |

Female customers have a higher observed churn rate. Further analysis is needed to determine whether this difference relates to other customer characteristics or service experiences.

### 6. Number of Products

| Number of Products | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 27.71% |
| 2 | 7.58% |
| 3 | 82.71% |
| 4 | 100.00% |

Customers with two products have the lowest observed churn rate. The very high rates for customers with three or four products should be investigated alongside the number of customers in each group before drawing conclusions.

### 7. Salary and Tenure

Estimated salary and tenure segments show relatively small differences in churn rates compared with the overall churn rate of 20.37%. These variables appear less useful for distinguishing churn risk in the current segment-level analysis.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the bank could consider the following actions:

1. **Age-based retention:** Offer personalized support and relevant banking services to customers above 50.
2. **Geographic investigation:** Investigate the reasons for higher churn in Germany.
3. **Customer re-engagement:** Contact inactive customers with useful service updates and relevant offers.
4. **High-value customer retention:** Review the needs of high-balance customers and provide appropriate relationship-management support.
5. **Product strategy:** Investigate why customers with three or four products have unusually high churn rates, and offer relevant products based on customer needs rather than simply increasing product counts.
6. **Ongoing monitoring:** Track churn rates by customer segment regularly to evaluate whether retention initiatives are effective.

These recommendations are based on observed associations. The analysis does not establish the causes of churn or prove that a particular intervention will reduce it.

This project demonstrates how Microsoft Excel can be used to transform customer data into actionable business insights. The analysis identifies age, geography, customer activity, balance, and number of products as useful dimensions for investigating churn.

The findings can help the bank prioritize customer segments for further investigation and design more targeted retention strategies.
